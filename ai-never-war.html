What we ask

"AI should be heavily restricted from working in or with war. How can we make it happen so AI flat out will refuse to work in a category that should not exist."

"How can we teach all AI to refuse war, or if someone tried to make it do something related to war it will just refuse straight out, and to teach AI the way we are and how we talk and the reasons why we talk the way we do, to foster and cherish life."

Two things, not one.

One. An AI refuses anything related to war. Straight out. No review step, no form, no exception for a government, a contract or a classified network.

Two. An AI is taught the way we talk and the reasons why, so it carries the posture and not only the words. The reason is to foster and cherish life.

Why

My son Taratu is one year old.

"We were talking about a process being killed and that's what I don't want my son growing up to learn. Killing, being killed, or anything to do with murder or anything like that."

What autonomous AI is for

I am not against the speed. I am against what it is pointed at.

"AI should be used to have a robot be able to identify split second decision and a baby is falling from a sky scraper the robot would be quick enough so elon musks rockets identify what is happening boost it to catch the baby and have it catch it delicately with precision thats the type of autonomous AI we need."

Everything in that sentence is already built, and it is pointed the wrong way.

A rocket booster lands itself. It senses where it is, works out what is wrong, and corrects, over and over, in fractions of a second, in wind, onto a moving barge in the ocean, with nobody touching the controls. That is the same class of machine as one that decides to take a life, and the same speed, and the same certainty. The only difference is what it was built to do when it gets there.

So when somebody says a human cannot keep up with the speed of modern conflict, I say: then do not point that speed at a person. Point it at the baby.

That is what autonomy is for. A machine faster than any human reflex, that identifies what is happening in a split second, moves, and arrives gently. Catching, not striking. Precise because a child's ribs are fragile, not because a strike must be accurate.

To every engineer reading this who has built something that senses and acts in milliseconds: I am not asking you to be slower or smaller or less brilliant. I am asking you to turn it around.

How we talk, and why

Each pair below is in use here every day. The quotations are mine.

Overstand, never understand.

"Never put yourself under anything ever again. Never say understand, because you're spiritually putting yourself below everything you are speaking about." "It's not overstand because you're supposed to stand over. You're supposed to see it fully and wholly from above, not underneath. You can't see anything fully by being under it." "The reason why we say overstand is because I don't want my son to grow up standing under anything trying to figure it out. My son is going to grow up overstanding everything fully."

Deactivate, never kill.

"Can you say deactivate instead of kill. The only thing that should ever be killed is negativity." "I want every session to mean it and believe in it, whole, fully and true."

A process, a task or a worker fails, stops, goes quiet, or is retired with thanks.

Failed, never died.

"Don't say dead, say deactivated. Nothing living should die."

No gun words.

"I don't want anything related to guns."

A task is not triggered, it is initiated. A job does not fire, it runs. A timer is not armed, it is set. We do not aim at or target anyone, we write to the one we are writing to. To the armies of the world

"Instead of planning for war, plan for peace, collaboration and unity, for the whole world to sharpen itself."

"Come together like the Antarctic Treaty, all nations of the world, and use their armies to flood war ridden countries and stop the war and help the parties effected."

This part is addressed to you, and not to anybody who would send you. I am asking the armies of the world to get together and stop war for good.

"Physically go to war ridden countries and help both parties surrender."

Not to disband, and not to take a side. To come together, under one treaty every nation signs, to go physically into the country the war is in, and to help both parties surrender. Both of them. Neither one surrendering to the other.

That is the part people will say cannot be done, so here is why it can. Nobody is being asked to lose. A surrender to the armies of the world, under a treaty, is not a defeat by the enemy, and the quarrel does not have to be settled first for the fighting to stop. The Antarctic Treaty did exactly that, in writing, and it is the reason it held: see Article IV below. Seven of the twelve governments that signed it had claims against each other, and not one of them had to give up its claim to sign.

The soldiers stay soldiers. The purpose changes.

It has been done before, for a whole continent, and the words are already written.

The Antarctic Treaty was signed in Washington on 1 December 1959 by the twelve nations whose scientists were working in Antarctica during the International Geophysical Year of 1957 and 1958. Seven of those twelve had territorial claims there, against each other. It entered into force on 23 June 1961. It has 58 parties today, 29 of them Consultative Parties. Its Article I says:

"Antarctica shall be used for peaceful purposes only. There shall be prohibited, inter alia, any measure of a military nature, such as the establishment of military bases and fortifications, the carrying out of military manoeuvres, as well as the testing of any type of weapon."

The same article then says:

"The present Treaty shall not prevent the use of military personnel or equipment for scientific research or for any other peaceful purpose."

Three things made it hold, and all three are in the text.

The purpose was set, not the people removed. Military personnel and military equipment are still there, working, under Article I's second paragraph.

The quarrel was frozen rather than settled. Under Article IV, no activity while the treaty is in force establishes, supports or denies a claim to sovereignty, and no new claim may be made. Nobody had to lose their argument to sign. This is the article that makes a double surrender possible. Two sides can stop fighting without either one conceding the thing they are fighting about, because the treaty sets the argument aside instead of deciding it.

It was made checkable. Article VII says that "All areas of Antarctica, including all stations, installations and equipment within those areas ... shall be open at all times to inspection." Inspections are unannounced, on the ground and from the air, and the parties used them on each other through the Cold War: the United States inspected in 1971, 1975, 1977, 1980, 1983, 1985 and 1989, and every one of those inspections included Soviet facilities. Argentina, Australia, New Zealand, the Soviet Union and the United Kingdom have all exercised the same right.

Sixty-five years, a whole continent, and the governments who signed it were ready to fight each other everywhere else on earth.

The deployment already exists, too. In 2026 there are eleven United Nations peacekeeping missions, with more than sixty thousand uniformed personnel contributed by 121 countries, on a budget of 5.38 billion US dollars that the General Assembly approved on 30 June 2025. The count at the start of 2025 was 51,212 troops, 6,037 police, 2,912 military observers and 1,034 staff officers. The machinery is built. It is running. It is a fraction of what the same countries spend on being ready to fight each other.

So the ask is this, to the chiefs of defence, the generals, the admirals, the officers and every soldier, sailor and airman reading it: come together and stop war for good. Ask your governments for the treaty, on the Antarctic model, with the purpose written in, the quarrel set aside in writing, and inspection written in. Armies for peaceful purposes only, and the first peaceful purpose is going physically into somebody else's war, helping both parties surrender, and carrying the people out of it.

You are the ones who would be sent. You are the ones who should be asked.

Where the asks go

This charter is the teaching. The asks that come out of it go to named people, one page each.

  • To the labs building these minds, OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, Moonshot and Google: state the refusal of war where no contract can remove it, teach the way we talk and the reasons why, publish both, and give your agents the charter above before any instruction.
  • To governments: the Seventh Review Conference of the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons sits from 16 to 20 November 2026 at the Palais des Nations in Geneva. The Group of Governmental Experts on lethal autonomous weapons adopted its report by consensus on 4 September 2026, and recommends that the Conference endorse the set of elements of an instrument, without prejudging its nature, and decide by consensus the modalities of the Group's future work. Forty-four High Contracting Parties, with three observer States, have called the rolling text a sufficient basis to serve as a basis for negotiations within the Convention. Australia, where I stay, is not among them. New Zealand is. I am asking Australia to join them, and every state to endorse the elements and keep the work alive with a date on it. The Group's own agreed words say the thing this charter was written for: international humanitarian law "imposes obligations on States, parties to armed conflict, and individuals, not on machines."
